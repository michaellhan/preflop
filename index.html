About the data

Preflop Lab trains you against a fixed 100bb, no-ante cash game equilibrium model. Every spot is a single decision: everyone before you has folded or made exactly one action, and you choose between fold, call/check and raise. The strategy for each of the 169 starting hands is stored as action frequencies (a hand can be a pure action or a mix such as "3-bet 40% / call 60%").

How accurate is it?

The ranges are hand-curated to match the published output of modern preflop solvers for this game model (the same family of solutions popularised by GTO Wizard, Simple Preflop, Monker and others). Range sizes, which hands are pure vs mixed, and the shape of each region (suited aces as 3-bet bluffs, suited connectors as calls, etc.) follow the solver consensus; mixed frequencies are rounded to 5-10% steps. They are not exported from any commercial product, so individual borderline hands can differ from a specific solver by a few percentage points, and different rake or sizing assumptions will move the edges of every range. If you train against a solver with different sizes, expect the edges to differ, not the core.

Heads-up note: heads-up is its own model, not a blind-vs-blind spot from 6-max. The small blind is the button and acts last postflop, so it opens or limps about 80% of hands and the big blind, out of position, defends with more 3-bets. The five heads-up spots (SB open with a limp option, BB vs open, SB vs 3-bet, BB vs limp, SB vs a raise after limping) have their own ranges.

9-max note: a 9-max seat with the same number of players left to act is structurally the same decision as its 6-max counterpart, so LJ/HJ/CO/BTN/SB opens reuse the 6-max strategies and UTG, UTG+1 and UTG+2 have their own tighter opening ranges. When an early-position player responds to an early-position open (e.g. LJ vs UTG), the app uses the tightest 6-max response (HJ vs UTG); these spots are marked in the chart footer.

How answers are scored

  • Correct: you picked the most frequent GTO action, or any action GTO takes at least 50% of the time. Worth 1 point.
  • Acceptable (mixed): GTO takes your action sometimes, but another action is the main line. Worth the action's frequency (e.g. 0.3 points for a 30% action).
  • Mistake: GTO never takes your action with that hand. 0 points. A mistake that costs an estimated 1bb or more is labelled a blunder.

Accuracy is points divided by hands. Stats are kept in your browser only.

EV loss

Every answer also shows an estimated EV loss in big blinds: how much your action costs compared with playing the GTO strategy, the same idea GTO Wizard's trainer reports. Two rules from game theory drive it:

  • At equilibrium, every action a hand mixes between has the same EV, so choosing any action GTO plays with that hand loses nothing. Only actions GTO never takes cost EV.
  • For those, EV loss = EV(GTO mix) − EV(your action), where EV(GTO mix) is the frequency-weighted EV of the actions GTO does take.

The EVs come from a compact preflop model built on the same ranges the trainer uses. Equity is computed by Monte Carlo simulation of your exact cards against the villain's real range (with card removal), and the standard fold-equity formulas are applied:

EV(raise) = P(fold) × pot  +  P(call) × (EQR × equity × final pot − invested)  +  P(re-raise) × EV(vs re-raise)
EV(call)  = EQR × equity × final pot − invested       (minus a squeeze penalty when players remain behind)
EV(fold)  = −(blinds or raises already invested)

EQR is equity realization: in position about 100%, out of position about 82%, +5% for the last aggressor, scaled by hand type (premium hands realize ~110%, weak offsuit hands ~80-85%, and non-premium hands lose a further 12-20% in 3-bet and 4-bet pots). The villain's fold/call/raise frequencies come from their ranges in this app; how a 3-bettor responds to a 4-bet, and how the SB responds when its limp is raised, are fixed approximations. Checked against the GTO ranges, the model picks the same action as GTO for about 80% of pure opening decisions and roughly 70% of the rest, so treat the numbers as a good ordering (folding AA costs far more than opening 72o) rather than a solver-exact value. Heads-up estimates are rougher still, because limp lines and very wide ranges stretch the simple model.

The "why" notes

After each hand (and when you hover a hand on the charts) a short note explains the decision: what kind of hand it is, why the position and the villain's range make the GTO action right, why GTO mixes when it does, and the general pattern it belongs to (why A5s 3-bets more than A9s, why small pairs fold to early opens but call late ones, and so on). The notes are rule-based, built from hand class, position, number of players behind and the opener's range, so they describe the standard solver reasoning rather than quoting a solver.

About "check"

Preflop, checking is only possible in the big blind when nobody has raised. The SB-limp scenario is the one place it shows up in a raise-or-fold model, so it is included as an optional scenario. Everywhere else your options are fold, call or raise.
